# Changelog

All notable changes to the `deliberate-engineering` plugin are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/), and the project aims at [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/) (pre-1.0: minor covers features and breaking changes, patch covers fixes).

## [0.13.0] - 2026-08-24

Composition patterns were second-class by omission. The override skill's own section titled "What is a target, and what is architecture" enumerates both categories, and patterns appeared in neither: not declared content, not declared architecture. Answering that question turned up something larger underneath it.

**A note on the version.** `CONTRIBUTING.md` makes a minor mean a new skill, command, catalog or lens, and this is none of those. It is a minor anyway, by judgment rather than by the letter: an adopter can now write `## review pattern #7: modify` in their override file, and could not before. Twenty-eight pieces of shipped content became addressable, which is a capability rather than a fix.

### Fixed
- **Every composition pattern was stated twice, and all four copies had drifted.** The patterns live in each catalog's Appendix, and each selector's Step 4 restated them under a lead-in of the form "apply the composition patterns from the catalog's Appendix". The two lists disagreed in all four phases. Nine patterns existed only in the Appendix and were absent from the selector that claimed to apply them, among them review's discovery-before-remediation (keep a review pass read-only, report and stop rather than fixing as you go), verification's promotion ladder and parity-over-argument, and planning's spike-before-you-plan-the-unknown. The patterns that did appear in both were renamed between them: "Lens rotation" against "Rotate the lens each pass", "No silent truncation" against "Never silently truncate". Each Step 4 now cites by number instead of restating, which is the repair the axis-honesty release used, for the same reason: copies drift, and these already had.
- **What was not deleted.** Several selector bullets carried phase-specific content the Appendix does not have, notably how debug-operate's evidence-before-action pattern resolves when the service is degraded, and the standing-rule reminders that are rules rather than patterns. Those stayed, now attached to the pattern number they extend rather than shadowing it.

### Added
- **Composition patterns are numbered and addressable.** Twenty-eight of them across four catalogs, each cited as `<catalog> pattern #N`, in a namespace of its own: `review #7` and `review pattern #7` are different content. The override layer names them as targets alongside lenses and standing rules, with the reasoning stated: a pattern prescribes how a phase's lenses chain, which is method rather than dispatch, so it is content on the same footing as a lens. The communication catalog keeps its prose composition note and takes no pattern targets, which is now a stated exception rather than an omission.
- **`contribute` and `promote` can carry one.** The candidate format gains `kind: lens | pattern`, and promotion knows the Appendix as a destination. This closes the gap in the direction that first exposed it: a candidate of this shape had been reshaped into a lens because the mechanism could not express it, and was then dropped at the gate for being a lens that duplicated two others.

### Tooling
- **Invariant 9 covers both bodies of numbered content.** Every composition pattern must be cited by its selector's compose step, the same claim already made for lenses, scoped to that step because elsewhere in a selector a bold number is a lens citation. It also fails when an appendix's patterns carry no numbers or are numbered out of sequence, and when a compose step has been renamed out from under them. The lens half gained one strip: a pattern citation is removed before lens citations are read, because in the review catalog lens 6 is loop-until-dry and pattern 6 is no-silent-truncation. 38 mutation controls, four of them new, and 4 positive controls.
- **`contribute` joins the stated consult exemptions.** Explaining the two namespaces required citing one, and the guard correctly flagged a skill that cites a lens number without applying lenses. It authors candidates rather than applying them, which is what its sibling `promote` is already exempt for.

## [0.12.3] - 2026-08-24

The plugin's most repeated defect, taken at the root. Sixteen review lenses were unreachable in practice: fourteen now route, two are deliberately exempt and say why, and a guard proves the property from now on instead of a reader eventually noticing it.

### Fixed
- **Eight lenses sat behind a frontend door.** One Step 3 bullet, keyed to "Frontend / mobile / infra / data / experiments", was the only pointer reaching both Part D ("Engineering-quality Lenses") and Part E ("Beyond Back-end"). Nothing else cited these eight, so a backend review with no frontend, mobile, infra, data or experiment angle reached none of them, although each states a trigger that is not one of those domains: 33 type-design ("Introducing new types/domain models"), 35 simplification / YAGNI ("After functionality is correct; before crystallizing a design"), 36 dependency / supply-chain ("When adding/updating libs"), 42 privacy / compliance ("Any change that collects, stores, moves, or exposes personal or financial data"), 45 cost, whose body ends "not only infrastructure changes", 46 disaster-recovery ("Critical services"), 49 internationalization ("Multi-region products") and 51 architecture critic ("Design proposals and significant refactors before implementation"). The group pointer now covers the nine Part E lenses its own five keys actually reach, and the eight route by number under the conditions their own bodies name.
- **Eight review lenses appeared nowhere in their selector at all.** Six now route: 8 judge panel into the risky band; 11 change-size / reviewability ("Any PR/CL") beside the always-first lens; 12 and 17 under a new trigger for semantics a reading cannot settle; 14 when more than one branch or worktree is in play; and 19 under its own trigger, an artifact contradicting an established norm, rather than under an axis its body never names. Lenses 5 ("Group decisions at risk of groupthink") and 7 ("Large teams, onboarding, areas prone to recurring oversights") stay unrouted on purpose, because both are built around a human organization that an agent-conducted review does not have. That is now a stated exemption in `scripts/routing-exemptions.txt` rather than a silence.
- **The worked example now obeys the steps above it.** It selected six lenses and logged one skipped group, while the band it declares names five lenses unconditionally and the step it illustrates had made four more so. An example is what an agent pattern-matches, so an example that quietly skips its own instructions teaches the skip. It now names its ceremony band, carries that band's pairs, and logs nine deliberate skips with a reason each.

### Changed
- **`promote` writes the selector, not only the catalog.** The flow edited the catalog, updated the counts, ran the reviewer and deleted the candidate, and never touched the file that routes the new lens. The instances fixed above came from hand-authored catalog commits rather than from that flow, but the flow would produce the same state by construction, and it is the one path where the gap can be closed instead of found later. Promotion now carries a routing step between the catalog edit and the review, and the candidate file is not deleted while its lens is unrouted, because deleting it is what makes the promotion look finished.

### Tooling
- **Invariant 9: every lens is cited by its own selector.** The exact inverse of invariant 2, which proved that every citation resolves to a lens and never that every lens is reachable from a citation. A citation must carry the lens's identity in one of three forms: its number beside a whole word from its own title, a numbers-only parenthesis, or the number introduced by the word lens. Frontmatter, headings, inline code spans, step and rule references, and another catalog's `review #30` are read as prose rather than as routing. A group-routed Part is declared in `scripts/routing-exemptions.txt` and must pin the exact set it blankets, so a lens appended to that Part later cannot join in silence; a heading that looks like a lens and is malformed fails rather than vanishing, which is what it did from both this guard and the count harness at once; and an exemption that has gone dead is itself a failure. 123 lenses, three stated exemptions, 34 mutation controls and 4 positive controls.
- **Controls that assert something works, not only that something fails.** Every control here had asserted a check fires, which is half a contract, and the missing half is what let a broken routing form ship: the regex for "lens N" parsed as "lense" plus an optional s, so the form the documentation named had never matched and nothing noticed. Each accepted citation form is now pinned by a control that turns red if that form breaks. Each exemption control also pins the message it expects, because six of them had been passing on a shared early branch rather than on the defect they name, and five of the guard's failure branches could be deleted with the suite still green.
- **What this guard does not prove.** It proves a lens is cited, not that the citation sits under a trigger a reviewer would match, and it can still be satisfied by a coincidence: a numeral standing near a whole word from a lens's title, or an ordinary parenthesis of digits. It does not parse block structure either, so a citation inside a code fence, an HTML comment or a table counts. An earlier version tried to strip those and did more harm than good, erasing twenty-eight lenses when a four-backtick fence paired with the next ordinary one. It also cannot tell a lens named in a routing bullet from one named in a skip log, and it looks for the lens's name only in the sixty characters after its number, so a real citation whose name sits further away is missed. The misses are the common case and the safe direction, and none of them survives the drift this guard exists for, which is a lens added and cited nowhere at all. Whether a citation is *good* routing stays a reading task.

## [0.12.2] - 2026-08-23

One lens amendment, contributed through the plugin's own `contribute` and `promote` flow run against itself, plus routing for two Part A lenses that no selector step reached.

### Changed
- **Review 4 now points inward first, and its title says so.** It was titled "Self-review of regressions introduced by your own fixes", and its method sent the reviewer outward, to what else the fix might have broken. The likelier defect is inward and same-class: a repair is written inside the frame that admitted the original defect, on the same subject matter and usually minutes later, so the branch added to handle an error has its own unhandled path and the sentence replacing a misleading one misleads in a new way. A same-class recurrence is a new defect in new text rather than a regression, which is why the title moved with the method. The lens keeps its number and its place.

### Fixed
- **Two of the six unrouted Part A lenses now route.** Lens 10 (completeness critic) and lens 54 (stopping criterion) were reachable only by reading the catalog straight through: no step of `review-strategy-selector` selected either one, and under progressive disclosure, where an agent reads only the sections a step names, that means never read. This is the defect class v0.4.0 fixed in the communication catalog and v0.4.1 fixed in four more lenses. Step 4 now routes both. **Deliberately not in this change:** lenses 5, 7, 8 and 11 carry the same defect and stay unrouted, lens 11 (change-size / reviewability, whose own scope is "any PR/CL") most visibly. Each wants a home chosen for it rather than a sweep appended to a patch, and two of six should not read as all of them.

## [0.12.1] - 2026-08-22

A cold measurement of the shipped 0.12.0 scored all three clauses of the architectural review's objective as met, and located eleven stale sentences anyway. This is those eleven, plus the guard that would have caught the sharpest of them.

### Fixed
- **The voice-build description stated a phase order its own body makes impossible.** It said the flow drives the interview and the blind A/B "then synthesizes the profile files", while the body runs interview, synthesize, A/B, and `bootstrap.md` requires a finished profile to compare the blind pick against. The command mirrored the description faithfully and was wrong with it. The guide had it right the whole time.
- **CONTRIBUTING sent readers to the README for the override-file format,** which lives in the architecture doc, from inside the section written to keep one home per topic. The link resolved and the content was not there, which is Rule 9's failure mode rather than a broken link.
- **The capture guide described the approval prompt as universal** and never mentioned the elevated confirmation a standing-rule candidate takes, nor capture's refusal to propose a safety-rail loosening from something it merely observed. That is the one behavior separating a constitutional change from a formatting preference, and it was missing from the page an adopter reads.
- **Smaller adherence repairs:** `promote`'s flow overview named three stages where its own file names four in three other places; the README's complete-disclosure list scoped two writes to a command that is not the only path to them, since the router and an orchestration both hand those over; the architecture doc stated the voice consult unconditionally where the selector excludes the code-agent audience, whose format a personal register would corrupt; three files called a documented refusal a warning, selling the safeguard short; the author flow named one of the two local checks the previous release shipped; the state note's by-construction isolation claim did not hold on a long-lived shared branch, where the branch name identifies the branch rather than the work unit; and the review catalog's Master Principle stated a composition rule where its four siblings state the phase's ruler.
- **The consistency harness failed cryptically on the platform default it warns about.** Run with macOS's `/bin/bash` (3.2) it died with `review: unbound variable`, naming the symptom and not the cause. It now says which bash it needs and how to run it.

### Tooling
- **The structural invariants are guarded mechanically, not only stated.** A reviewer proved by mutation that the existing harness was blind to everything that is not a count: renumbering an entire catalog passed green, deleting the override consult from all five selectors passed, a sixth catalog was invisible because the catalog list was a hardcoded array, and a thirteenth command passed while the README said twelve. `scripts/check-invariants.py` now guards seven invariants: a lens number is a permanent address and may never disappear between the base revision and HEAD; every `<catalog> #N` and `Rule N` citation resolves to a real heading (214 of them today); every skill that applies a numbered lens consults the override layer; no catalog is invisible to the count harness and no command invokes a skill that does not exist; every named-section reference resolves; passages the text claims are identical are identical; and no doubled word or conflict marker ships.
- **Every guard has a negative control, and they run in CI.** `scripts/test-check-invariants.py` copies the repository, breaks one invariant at a time, and asserts the right check fails, because a check nobody has watched fail is not a check. Twelve mutations, each caught by its own check. Two of them found blind spots in the guards themselves while they were being written: the consult check was satisfied by the two strings appearing anywhere in a file rather than by an actual consult, and it never examined the five selectors at all, because a selector cites its own lenses as bare numbers rather than as `review #25`.
- **Exemptions are stated, not silent.** Three skills cite lens numbers without applying them (`promote` edits catalogs, `state` records what a selector already chose, `capture` writes override entries that address them). Each is listed in `scripts/consult-exemptions.txt` with its reason, and the guard fails on an exemption with no reason or for a skill that does not exist.
- **An eighth invariant: every relative link and anchor resolves.** Rule 9 forbids shipping a pointer the reader cannot resolve, and nothing checked the plugin's own. 103 links and anchors, all resolving, with two negative controls (a missing file, an anchor matching no heading). The citation guard also now reads the CHANGELOG, which carries 53 of the 267 citations it verifies and had been outside its scope.
- **The version gate covers `plugins/` rather than one plugin directory**, so a second plugin is gated from the day it lands instead of shipping silently exempt.

**What this does not do:** make coherence self-enforcing. It stops the mechanical invariants from regressing. The semantic ones (a description that hides a write, a gloss that misreads its lens, a claim untrue of a sibling) remain the work of review, and the record of this release says that work wants fresh context and more than one angle.

## [0.12.0] - 2026-08-22

The override-reach release: the second half of the architectural review's findings, where the override layer promised more reach than its plumbing had. Five verified majors shared one root, and they are fixed together because they are one system.

### Changed
- **The override layer now reaches every skill that applies a lens, and standing rules arrive before the work does.** The consult was a one-line pointer living in exactly six files, while `deliberate-engineering-conduct` (fourteen numbered lenses at its contract fields), `deliberate-engineering-orchestrate` (the planning calibrate-and-decompose lenses, applied one level up) and the router's ceremony-band step (which cites planning #10) applied lenses and consulted nothing. The plugin's own shipped example was the sharpest case: `## planning #12: modify` was honored under `/deliberate-engineering:plan` and silently dropped under `/deliberate-engineering:conduct`, whose step-order field applies planning #12 verbatim.

  The trigger model is now split by layer, because the two directions fail differently. **Standing rules** (`Rule N` overrides and `add: rules`) are read **once at the start of a session by the rules skill**, the layer in play for the whole session rather than for one phase, since that direction fails *open*: an operator-authored rule nobody reads is not a loosened rule, it is an absent one, and the failure is silent. **Catalog lenses** stay caller-owned and on demand, since a `disable` or `modify` fails *closed* when unread and a lens matters only once selected, but "the caller" now means every skill that applies one, not only the five that select them. The skill is explicit that an operator-authored `add: <catalog>` lens is the exception that fails open like a standing rule, why it stays on demand regardless, and what residual gap that leaves. This also resolves the contradiction inside the override skill, which said both that it fires whenever a lens is about to be applied and that it never decides for itself whether to run: each sentence now has its own scope.
- **The consult lands where the lens is actually applied.** `conduct` consults once as the contract is authored, before the gate table is fixed, and records in the conductor doc which lenses were overridden and how, so a resumed session inherits the calibration; it does not re-ask per step, which would re-open a settled question mid-cascade. `orchestrate` consults at decomposition and carries what fired into each handoff, in a new contract field, so a worker session inherits the operator's calibration instead of missing it. The router consults at its band step.
- **The router exemption is restated on its real ground.** It said the router is not a target because it "applies no lenses of its own", which its own Step 3 falsifies. The exemption was always about two objects, not about the skill: the four routing axes and the genre to phase-sequence map are the classification the whole dispatch rests on, so they stay architecture and out of reach. Where the router does apply a cited lens, that lens's override now applies there too, which matters because planning #10 is the catalog lens `capture` proposes for a recurring calibration (its other route for one is `add: rules`, which the session-start read now covers).
- **The contribution queue accepts what the plugin actually ships.** `communication` joins the addressable target set on every side (the override file's reading contract, `capture`, `contribute`, `promote`), closing a gap where the communication catalog advertised citable numbered lenses that no write path could address. A `rules` candidate class now exists and is **structural by construction**: `promote` routes it to a full design cycle, never to the append-only catalog edit, because a standing rule is constitutional content every session loads. CONTRIBUTING no longer promises a rule-change route the queue could not hold.

### Fixed
- **The write side can no longer frame a safety rail as a formatting preference.** `deliberate-engineering-capture` could author `Rule N` and `add: rules` overrides while carrying no safety vocabulary at all, presenting every candidate with the same "Approve this candidate, edit it, or reject it?". Those two targets now take an elevated step: name it as a standing-rule change in force from the start of every future session, translate it into the behavior it permits ("I will merge to shared branches in staging without stopping for you", not "Rule 1: modify"), and take a confirmation distinct from the ordinary three-way prompt. And one thing the skill will no longer do at all: it never *initiates* a loosening of a safety rail (Rule 1's human gate or Rule 2's read-only posture) from an observed deviation, judged by what an entry would permit rather than by how it is addressed, so an `add: <catalog>` lens whose body says "skip the gate here" is caught the same way. Seeing a gate skipped twice is not evidence the operator wants it gone. It names the pattern and stops; a gate an agent can remove by inference is not a gate.
- **The shipped capture template taught an override the override layer declares invalid.** Its worked calibration block produced a `planning #10: modify` entry saying "skip the plan phase", which is the router's genre to phase-sequence map, declared non-overridable, and which was delivered to the planning selector anyway, running after the sequence was already fixed. The template now stays inside the lens it targets, and capture's carve-out names the sequence map alongside the axes.
- **Two override-format contradictions the plugin shipped against its own example.** "Append-only" was carrying two meanings at once: the shipped text is never rewritten (file mechanics, which is what keeps an override alive as a lens evolves) and, read literally, an annotation could never change behavior. The plugin's own example is a narrowing (`planning #12: modify`, "verify the sequence only at the aggregate boundary"), so the rule contradicted the example beside it. The annotation now governs where the two conflict, and the declaration says what changed. Separately, the reading contract required `**When:**` on every `add` entry while the skill's own `add: rules` example omitted it and the ambiguity rule turned its absence into a stop-and-ask, which would have met the new session-start read with a question instead of the operator's safety line. A standing rule holds unconditionally, so `**When:**` is optional for `add: rules` and is read as scope when present.
- **`disable` on a lens removes a method, never a required contract field.** Where a skill's contract requires an artifact and cites a lens for how to produce it, disabling the lens leaves the obligation standing: the conductor contract still requires a verified recovery path whether or not verify #14 is disabled, and the skill says so instead of reporting the field satisfied or dropping it quietly.
- **Rule 1's boundary around `commit` is drawn once.** The rules skill placed the gate at merge, deploy, tag and push while other skills treated committing itself as gated or as ordinary. The rule now states it plainly: a commit on a local branch nobody else has is neither irreversible nor outward-facing, and Rule 6 in fact asks for it; what the gate forbids is committing onto a shared branch or as the step that carries work past a boundary the operator has not opened. A skill that stops earlier (the catalog write in `promote`) is a stricter local stance, not a different reading.
- **The declaration protocol no longer contradicts itself on permission.** It said a safety-loosening override is acknowledged rather than asked about, and elsewhere that it takes a confirmation check. The confirmation belongs at authoring time, in `capture`, and the read side acknowledges with a standing invitation to interrupt, which is why the one confirmation has to be real.
- **The direct-entry path is wired like the routed one.** The plugin sells two ways in, the front door and calling a phase yourself, and eight of twelve commands take the second. Only the first was finished: measured across the five selectors, none mentioned the standing rules, the checkpoint write, re-classification when the work changes, or the communication layer, because the router had been carrying all four. So a `Rule 2: modify` override was honored under `/deliberate-engineering:start` and silently dropped under `/deliberate-engineering:review`, a `/deliberate-engineering:verify` that turned into an incident had nothing to re-classify it, and the phase handling an incident could not reach the layer that writes the message about it. The four phase selectors now carry the same paragraph, byte-identical, the shape that already worked for the override consult; the communication selector carries the version that is true of it, since it is not a phase and is itself the layer the others are told to consult.
- **Three catalog bands were unreachable from the step that selects them.** Verification's route question named four kinds of check against five catalog Parts, orphaning the promotion Part; planning's selection list had no trigger for its disambiguation band, so a foggy requirement never reached the lenses written for it; and the router named the build step as delegated without saying what happens when no engine is installed, which is that it happens anyway under the same rules and gates.
- **Two words that meant several things now mean one each, with an owner.** A third, "ritual", was a load-bearing field name in the state note and a third word for a lens; it is now called what it is, the chosen lenses. "Ruler" carried two genuinely different concepts: the depth ruler (the plugin's headline claim, that depth follows the cost of being wrong) and the router's section on which phases it will and will not name. The depth ruler is now owned by `deliberate-engineering-rules`, stated once as the thesis it already carried, and everyone else states it in their own phase's terms or cites it; the router's colliding section is renamed. And the band ran under three names, `ceremony band` in the router, state, rules, conduct and overrides, `depth band` in three selectors and `planning depth` in the fourth, which meant the value a selector emitted never matched the schema field it was written into. One name now: `ceremony band`, one concept at two altitudes, with the router saying so and the same three values (minimal / standard / full) at both.
- **The architecture doc described the plugin as of three commits earlier.** An end-to-end audit of the concepts document, the one surface that had produced a finding in every prior pass, returned twelve. Its root cause is this release's own history: the commit that rewrote nine descriptions to disclose their writes updated the README and the flow guide and never touched this file. It mapped fourteen of sixteen skills, and the two it omitted are the two with no slash command, so the map inherited the command surface's blind spot; one of the two writes into the reader's repository. It claimed the override file "takes precedence over the shipped content" sixteen lines after correctly stating the limit; it said nothing is forced but the human gate, when the live-note write is equally unconditional; it undercounted the personal layers, stated a best-effort leak check as a guarantee, gave the voice layer one entry point where it has two, and credited promote with one write target where it has six. All twelve are fixed. Two of the three diagrams were structurally false, which is where a wrong claim is hardest to notice: the phase diagram omitted the altitude check that runs before classification and both routes it opens, and the extensibility diagram showed promote's single write and not the queue deletion. All three now render, verified through mermaid-cli.
- **The communication selector was the one member still shipped at draft weight.** An end-to-end audit of the only selector no pass had ever owned found five defects: it routed every non-operator reader to the voice layer, including the `code-agent` audience that the voice skill explicitly refuses; its terminal step omitted three obligations the same file states elsewhere, so following the numbered steps produced a draft with no target named and no stop; it described two selection mechanisms that disagree, with a tag field that cannot discriminate the two lenses the map treats as conditional; it promised to cover a reply to a review comment and routed only the comment, sending the reply to the catch-all and away from the lens whose value peaks on disagreement; and it enumerated tickets, chat messages, emails, invites and stakeholder messages as first-class while no lens carries substance for any of them. The first four are fixed. For the fifth, the skill now says plainly what the catalog supplies for those artifacts (register and resolvability) and what it does not (substance), so a thin selection reads as a deliberate floor instead of a full pass.
- **Nine of sixteen skills hid a write.** The plugin states its own rule that the description is the disclosure mechanism, so a capability behind a wrong description is inert; an end-to-end audit of all sixteen descriptions and all twelve commands found it did not keep that rule. The descriptions now say what each skill does to your files: `promote` edits the counts, both manifests and the CHANGELOG that its catalog edit forces, and deletes the candidate it promotes; `orchestrate` commits the tracker as each disposition lands; `conduct` writes a conductor doc and updates the tracker; the router writes a live note at every phase transition, which reaches `.deliberate/state/` and can add that path to `.gitignore`; the always-on rules skill binds that write to every checkpoint and treats a local commit as ordinary work; `capture` extracts the operator's typed transcript to a temporary directory it does not delete; `contribute` creates `candidates/` and a README inside the plugin's own clone rather than in your project.
- **Two catalogs advertised less than they hold.** The review selector said "code review" while its catalog reaches accessibility, infrastructure, CI/CD, privacy, cost, disaster recovery, internationalization and experiments, and reviews specs, designs and architectures as readily as code; the planning selector never mentioned its disambiguation band. Both say so now.
- **The voice layer and the communication selector both fired first.** Their descriptions enumerated near-identical artifact lists, so "draft my PR description" matched both at full strength, and voice winning that race inverts the plugin's own precedence (substance before surface). Each description now states its position in the order.
- **An operator-authored lens could never be reached.** Every consult pointer in the plugin was worded "if any **selected lens** has an operator override", and an `add: <catalog>` entry maps to no shipped lens number, so no applier could find one. An operator could write their own lens, watch the block land in the file, and never once have it applied or mentioned. All eight consult sites now ask for `add:` entries explicitly, as a separate step from looking up the lenses that were selected.
- **The always-on recipe now carries the read it is relied on for.** The README block instructed the router imperatively and described the rules skill only in passing, while two skills had begun to state the session-start standing-rule read as though it were guaranteed. The block now instructs that read, the skills state the limit honestly, and the README no longer claims the always-on core is just the nine rules, which stopped being true when standing-rule overrides joined it.
- **Contributors are told about the checks their own path trips.** Following `promote`'s append-only route exactly as written produced four consistency-harness failures, because neither the skill nor CONTRIBUTING mentioned the counts that several documents state or the script that enforces them; the script was referenced nowhere a contributor reads. Both now name it and enumerate the obligations, so the counts are updated in the same edit rather than discovered from a red run.
- **A conduction and its work unit no longer claim the same durable home.** The conductor doc holds the cluster's own state (the station table, the current gate, its pendings); the work unit's phase, sequence, ceremony band and chosen lenses stay wherever they already lived, since a baton does not dissolve the work unit. `deliberate-engineering-state` and `deliberate-engineering-conduct` now state that split in the same words, in both directions.
- **A work unit with no branch, PR or ticket can be named.** `deliberate-engineering-state` keyed identity to those three, which a voice-profile build (outside any repository) has none of, as `voice-build` itself pointed out. A short assigned-name fallback now gives such a unit stable identity across sessions.

## [0.11.0] - 2026-08-21

The coherence release: the outcome of a full architectural review of the plugin (a structural map, five parallel judgment passes over ten criteria, then an adversarial verification pass that refuted or downgraded most of what the first pass found). Everything below survived that verification. No lens was added, removed, or renumbered; all counts are unchanged.

### Changed
- **The plugin no longer claims its phase selectors share one set of four axes.** It was asserted outright at the router's Step 1, the planning selector's ruler line and the communication selector's opening, implied at the override skill's classification note, the README, the architecture doc and CONTRIBUTING, and restated in router-scoped form in several more places that are now attributed explicitly rather than left to read as universal. It was false for two of the four: verification classifies on evidence type, irreversibility of being wrong, environment reach, and stake; debug-operate opens by asking whether a reliable expectation exists at all. The divergence is principled, since verification starts from an expectation already stated and debug-operate starts where none exists, so the claim changed rather than the selectors. The router now states the split once, and the other sites cite it instead of keeping their own copies. What the plugin actually shares, and now says it shares, is the **ruler**: the cost of being wrong, not the size of the change.
- **Verification and debug-operate stopped presenting a route-selector as an ordinal axis.** Each numbered a nominal question (which catalog Part applies; is there a reliable expectation) as the first member of a classification list, debug-operate calling the list "four axes" outright, which their own later text already contradicted. Each now separates the route-selector or entry gate from its depth axes, and the worked examples follow the new numbering.

## [0.7.0] - 2026-08-20

### Added
- **`deliberate-engineering-orchestrate`: a cross-session orchestrator.** The across-session sibling of the router. Where the router conducts the phases *within* one session, this orchestrates the units of work *across* sessions, for a program too large to fit a single context. It runs one loop: plan → dispatch decision → a self-contained handoff with its premise frozen against a live-state read → execute (a fresh, human-watched session by default) → WORK REPORT → verify at source, in a *separate* context for contested or high-stakes claims and at a scope as wide as the claim → disposition (accept/reject/follow-up) → a self-auditing tracker with a read-back → commit → capture any operator-caught gap. The worker is then done; a follow-up is a new self-contained handoff, never a kept-alive session. It is manual-first (Rule 8): a fresh session is the default dispatch, and a background subagent is an earned exception, allowed only for a unit that is self-contained and analytical/mechanical, has no stall-able access/tool/MCP prerequisite, is low-stakes and reversible, and ends in no outward action, widening only as trust converges and gated on a token budget. It is deliberately thin: roughly two-thirds of the loop is Rules 1/3/6/8/9 at a larger granularity, cited rather than restated, and it reuses the selectors (a worker runs its own plan/review/verify; the orchestrator's verify is the verification selector, its disposition the review selector), delegates the durable store to `deliberate-engineering-state` (the program tracker *is* the store that skill delegates to, not a second one), and routes its comms through `communication-collaboration-selector` and `deliberate-engineering-voice`. Placement was the open call: a DE capability rather than a standalone skill, because standalone would duplicate the rules, state, and selectors and drift out of sync with them, the one failure mode the design most wanted to avoid.
- **The orchestration templates.** `templates.md` beside the skill carries the three contracts, read on demand rather than up front: a handoff (mandate, premise freeze, exact scope, gates, verification steps, required report format, an explicit DO-NOT list), a work report (new state, what changed, gate/test evidence, judgment calls, an explicit "not done per dispatch"), and a program tracker with an always-current recovery anchor that lets any fresh session re-instantiate the orchestrator. They prescribe the *fields* (the part that made real handoffs work) and leave the *layout* free; the orchestrator generates the mechanical fields from a live-state read and hand-writes only the judgment fields. No program content ships: the plugin carries the mechanism and the skeletons, and every program's tracker lives in the operator's own private repo, the same split the voice layer already uses.
