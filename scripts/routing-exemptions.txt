# Lenses reachable from their selector without a number citation, and lenses
# deliberately left unrouted, each with its reason. An exemption is a stated
# decision: one that belongs here for a bad reason is a finding, and one that is
# missing entirely is a defect the check will catch.

# Format: group <skill-directory> <Part X>: <reason>
#         lens  <skill-directory> <N>: <reason>
#
# A group exemption must open its reason with 'covers <numbers>.' naming exactly the
# lenses it blankets, so a lens appended to that Part later cannot join it in silence.

group review-strategy-selector Part E: covers 37-41, 43, 44, 47-49. Step 3 routes what remains of this group whole ("the matching Part-E group; open only the relevant subsection") because each of the ten it covers states a trigger that IS a domain: accessibility, responsiveness, SPA state, mobile lifecycle, device compatibility, IaC, CI/CD, data pipelines, experiments, i18n. Five members of the Part are not domain surfaces and route by number instead: 42, whose trigger is personal or financial data; 45, whose body says "not only infrastructure changes"; 46, whose trigger is a service's criticality; 50, finalizing a spec or plan; and 51, a design proposal or a boundary move. Ten covered plus those five is the Part's full fifteen.

lens review-strategy-selector 5: devil's advocate fires on "group decisions at risk of groupthink", which presupposes a human group to rotate the role through. An agent-conducted review has none, and the adversarial stance it would contribute is already routed as lens 2.

lens review-strategy-selector 7: checklist-based review fires on "large teams, onboarding, areas prone to recurring oversights", the first two properties of a human organization rather than of the change under review. The third is a property of the codebase and would be routable on its own, but the lens as written is built around a shared standing checklist that a team maintains, which an agent-conducted review has no equivalent of. Routing the whole lens would push org ceremony into a per-change classification.
