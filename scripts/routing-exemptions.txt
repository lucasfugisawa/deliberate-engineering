group review-strategy-selector Part E: covers 37-41, 43, 44, 47, 48. Step 3 routes what remains of this group whole ("the matching Part-E group; open only the relevant subsection") because each of the nine it covers is reached by one of the five keys that pointer names: accessibility, responsiveness and SPA state by frontend; mobile lifecycle and device compatibility by mobile; IaC and CI/CD by infra; data pipelines by data; experiments by experiments. Six members of the Part are reached by no key and route by number instead: 42, whose trigger is personal or financial data; 45, whose body ends "not only infrastructure changes"; 46, whose trigger is a service's criticality; 49, whose trigger is a multi-region product; 50, finalizing a spec or plan; and 51, a design proposal or a boundary move. Nine covered plus those six is the Part's full fifteen.

lens review-strategy-selector 5: devil's advocate fires on "group decisions at risk of groupthink", which presupposes a human group to rotate the role through. An agent-conducted review has none, and the adversarial stance it would contribute is already routed as lens 2.

lens review-strategy-selector 7: checklist-based review fires on "large teams, onboarding, areas prone to recurring oversights", the first two properties of a human organization rather than of the change under review. The third is a property of the codebase and would be routable on its own, but the lens as written is built around a shared standing checklist that a team maintains, which an agent-conducted review has no equivalent of. Routing the whole lens would push org ceremony into a per-change classification.
